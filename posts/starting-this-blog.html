INTRODUCTION · 3 MIN READ

Why I’m writing about engineering

A professional space to share technical ideas, reflect on lessons learned, and explain the engineering concepts I find useful.

Some of the most useful lessons in software engineering live between the lines of a design document and the final implementation. They emerge while defining an API contract, tracing an unexpected failure, simplifying a workflow, or deciding how a service should behave when something goes wrong.

I’m creating this site to collect and explain those lessons. Writing helps me test whether I understand an idea well enough to make it clear to someone else—and gives me a place to return to as my perspective develops.

What you’ll find here

I plan to write about backend engineering, API design, cloud infrastructure, distributed workflows, automation, and professional growth. Posts will focus on transferable ideas and practical approaches rather than internal details from any particular employer.

Learning in public, thoughtfully

This is not a claim to have every answer. It is a record of questions worth exploring, patterns that help, and concepts I’m working to understand more deeply. My goal is to be specific enough to be useful, while being careful about accuracy and context.

All views here are my own. I’ll keep the content independent of employer-confidential information, private customer details, and proprietary implementation specifics.

Thanks for reading.

— Keerthana
